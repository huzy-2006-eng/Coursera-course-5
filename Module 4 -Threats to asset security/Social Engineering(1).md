# Social Engineering:
 1. A manipulation technique that exploits human error to gain private informatin,access or valuables.

 2. Social engineering is a form of deception that takes advantage of the way people think. It preys on people’s natural feelings of curiosity, generosity, and excitement.

 3. Threat actors turn those feelings against their targets by affecting their better judgment. Social engineering attacks can be incredibly harmful because of how easy they can be to accomplish.

 4. One of the highest-profile social engineering attacks that occurred in recent years was the Twitter Hack of 2020. During that incident, a group of hackers made phone calls to Twitter employees pretending to be from the IT department. Using this basic scam, the group managed to gain access to the organization’s network and internal tools. This allowed them to take over the accounts of high-profile users, including politicians, celebrities, and entrepreneurs.

**Stages:**
1. Prepare.
2. Establish Trust.
3. Use persuasion tactics.
4. Disconnect from the target.

**Preventing social engineering:**
1. Implementing managerial controls.
2. Staying informed of trends.
3. Sharing your knowledge with others.

# Types of social engineering attacks:
1. **Baiting** is a social engineering tactic that tempts people into compromising their security. A common example is a USB baiting that relies on someone finding an infected USB drive and plugging it into their device.

2. **Phishing** is the use of digital communications to trick people into revealing sensitive data or deploying malicious software. It is one of the most common forms of social engineering, typically performed via email.

3. **Quid pro quo** is a type of baiting used to trick someone into believing that they'll be rewareded in reutrn for sharing accesss,information or money. For example, an attacker might impersonate a loan officer at a bank and call customers offering them a lower interest rate on their credit card. They'll tell the customers that they simply need to provide their account details to claim the deal.

4. **Tailgating** is a social engineering tactic in which unauthorized people follow an authorized person into a restricted area. This is also referrred to as piggybacking.

5. **Watering hole** is a type of attack when a threat actor compromises a website frequently used by a group of users. Oftentimes, these watering hole sites are infected with malicious software. An example is the HOLY WATER ATTACK OF 2020 that infected various religious,charity and volunteer websites.

# Encouraging caution:
1. **Stay alert** of suspicious communication and unknown people, especially when it comes to email. For example,look out for spelling errors and double-check the sender's name and email address.

2. **Be cautious** about sharing information, especially over social media. Threat actors often search these platforms for any information they can use to their advantage.

3. **Control curiosity**  when something seems too good to be true. This can include wanting to click on attachments or links in emails and advertisements.

# Early persuasion tactics:
1. One of the earliest instances of phishing was aimed at a popular chat service called AOL Instant Messenger (AIM). Users of the service began receiving emails asking them to verify their accounts or provide personal billing information. The users were unaware that these messages were sent by malicious actors pretending to be service providers.

2. This was one of the first examples of mass phishing, which describes attacks that send malicious emails out to a large number of people, increasing the likelihood of baiting someone into the trap.

3. During the AIM attacks, malicious actors carefully crafted emails that appeared to come directly from AOL. The messages used official logos, colors, and fonts to trick unsuspecting users into sharing their information and account details.

# Types of phishing:
1. **Email phishing** is a type of attack sent via email in which threat actors send messages pretending to be a trusted person or entity.
2. **Smishing**  is a type of phishing that uses Short Message Service (SMS), a technology that powers text messaging. Smishing covers all forms of text messaging services, including Apple’s iMessages, WhatsApp, and other chat mediums on phones.
3. **Vishing** refers to the use of voice calls or voice messages to trick targets into providing personal information over the phone.
4. **Spear phishing** is a subset of email phishing in which specific people are purposefully targeted, such as the accountants of a small business.
5. **Whaling** refers to the category of spear phishing attempts that are aimed at high-ranking executives in an organization.

6. **Angler phishing** is a technique where attackers impersonate customer service respresentatives on social media.
A. This tactic evolved from people’s tendency to complain about businesses online. 
B. Threat actors intercept complaints from places like message boards or comment sections and contact the angry customer via social media. Like the AIM attacks of the 1990s, they use fraudulent accounts that appear similar to those of actual businesses. They then trick the angry customers into sharing sensitive information with the promise of fixing their problem.
